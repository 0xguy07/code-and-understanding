# Code and Understanding

I'm rebuilding my foundations in **software development** and **artificial intelligence**, and documenting the process through code, notes, projects, and reflection.

**[View the curriculum →](CURRICULUM.md)**

## Why I'm doing this

Over the last few years, I've gone from chatting with AI in a web browser to using it through the **command-line interface (CLI)** with tools such as **Claude Code**, **Codex**, and **Gemini**, along with local language models through **Ollama**.

I've used AI to build tools for work and hobbies. It has helped me explore ideas and solve problems that would have taken me much longer to tackle on my own. Along the way, though, I've also become increasingly aware of a gap between what I can produce with AI assistance and what I can write, explain, or rebuild independently.

It's easy to fall into a cycle of asking AI for an implementation, giving it the next error, and repeating until something works. A working program can arrive with very little practice at writing the code or deciding what to try next. I've fallen into that pattern myself.

As I explore AI more deeply, I want to strengthen the fundamentals underneath it. I want to understand the systems I'm building, recognize when an answer is wrong, and make informed decisions about the code I use.

## How I'll learn with AI

I think AI can be a valuable learning partner. At work, I often use it to help find answers directly because I need to get to the root cause of a problem quickly. Here, I'm making room to work through the problem and develop the skills involved.

I'll follow the coursework, complete exercises, and use AI as a tutor for additional explanations, feedback, and practice. My intended habits are to:

- Make an attempt and write down where I'm stuck before asking for help.
- Ask for hints and explanations that help me take the next step.
- Test the code and explain why it behaves as it does.
- Revisit exercises independently and try variations on the original problem.
- Record meaningful AI assistance and what I learned from it in project notes.

These are habits I'm working to develop. My notes will include the times I struggle with them, too.

## What I hope to gain

Over an initial three-to-six-month curriculum, I want to become more confident at:

- Turning a problem into a program using Python.
- Reading unfamiliar code, diagnosing errors, and writing useful tests.
- Working with Git, databases, and APIs.
- Understanding how AI models work and evaluating their output.
- Making deliberate decisions about when and how to use AI assistance.

For employers, educators, and other learners, I want this repository to offer evidence of that development. Exercises, project histories, tests, and explanations will show how my understanding changes over time. I'll distinguish guided coursework from independent projects and document what I can do on my own.

This repository will grow to include subject notes, exercises, projects, reading reflections, and ideas for future work. I'll keep the questions, mistakes, and revisions that help explain how I arrived at a solution.

My learning draws on **KodeKloud** and **Zero To Mastery**. I'm starting with **GIT for Beginners** on KodeKloud, followed by Python, databases, APIs, and practical AI development. The [curriculum](CURRICULUM.md) holds the course links, milestones, and progress.

## Reading and reflection

My reading has given me more to think about as I use and study AI. Questions about human agency, learning, and the wider consequences of automation are part of my motivation for understanding these systems more deeply.

I've finished Ethan Mollick's *Co-Existence*. I'm also working through Kevin Roose's *The AGI Chronicles* and Jill Lepore's *The Rise and Fall of the Artificial State*. Gil Durán's *The Nerd Reich* is another influence on the questions I'm considering.

As I develop reading notes, I'll document specific ideas, disagreements, and open questions alongside the technical work. Reading a book is an opportunity to examine its arguments and consider how they affect my own choices.

## For other learners

If you've found yourself handing more of the thinking to AI than you intended, you may recognize parts of this journey. I hope seeing my attempts, mistakes, and progress helps you find a starting point for your own learning.

I'm beginning with Git, then building small Python programs and working toward applications I can understand, test, and improve myself.
